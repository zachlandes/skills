# jev-field-practice: sources

The public studies behind the numbers in `SKILL.md`.
Load this when you need a number's source, its sample size or model version, or want to check whether a finding still holds.

None of these are our measurements, and we have re-run none of them.
E1-E13 were gathered from the curated list [Awesome Jev Robustness](https://github.com/Yifan-Lan/awesome-jev-robustness) as it stood on 2026-09-23, when almost every entry was a one-person study run on `jev-1.13.0` in the two weeks after release.
"Opened" means we read the study's own README; "list" means the number is the list's summary and we did not open the study.
"Bears on" says which heuristic the study supports, refines or challenges.

TypeSafe's own pages are cited by link in `SKILL.md` and are not repeated here.

| ID | Finding | Bears on | Source (n, model) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Without an explicit unknown or none option Jev answers anyway: removing the abstain option took accuracy on unanswerable items from 0.950 to 0.000, at 0.79 confidence. 0 of 30 out-of-scope messages were flagged without a "none of these" option, at 0.99. Asked for a page element that does not exist, it answered `none` at 1.0 and 0.92 when `none` was offered. | Agrees with giving every Choice a no-op or abstain option. | [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), about 7,000 calls, 1.13.0, opened; [priorbench/jev](https://github.com/priorbench/jev), 5,721 calls, 1.13.0, pre-registered, opened; [jevnav](https://github.com/dtduc-git/jevnav), 44 decisions, 1.13.0, opened |
| **E2** | Many questions over one state do not interfere: 16 questions against 1 shifted confidence by 0.008 and flipped 0.4% of answers, also with 5 hostile neighbouring questions. 13 questions batched against 13 separate calls gave identical answers, with flat latency from 1 to 30 questions. | Agrees with one request per screen. | [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), 250 items, 1.13.0, opened; [finnhll/jev-eval](https://github.com/finnhll/jev-eval), 282 trials, 1.13.0, opened |
| **E3** | Many independent items in one state do interfere: 40 rows in one state of about 12.6k tokens moved the later rows by about 0.42 and flipped 77 of 360 decisions; one row per request passed. 40 Korean sentences scored 40 of 40 one per call and 62% as one document. | Refines one request per screen: the win is many questions over one screen, not many items packed into one state. | [jev-orderby-bench](https://github.com/yodablocks/jev-orderby-bench), 360 rows, 1.13.0, pre-registered, opened; [Threads post](https://www.threads.com/@ebrain.lab/post/DddGgXuoLlL), 40 sentences, list |
| **E4** | A Choice's confidence is not a separate signal: it is (N·p_max − 1)/(N − 1) over N options, the same formula TypeSafe's confidence page gives. | Refines floors: a 0.5 confidence floor demands a top probability of 0.75 on a two-option Choice but only about 0.51 on a 50-option one, and adding `none` raises N (our arithmetic, not a measurement). | [primeline.cc pre-registered test](https://primeline.cc/blog/typesafe-jev-pre-registered-test), about 9,750 calls, list |
| **E5** | In a browser goal loop, correct decisions landed at probabilities from 0.41 to 0.99 and wrong ones from 0.39 to 0.47, so probability did not separate them; deterministic guards kept the loop safe. Single element-selection decisions on real pages: 41 of 41 right, 30 of 30 at 0.9 or above. | Agrees with stopping loops in code; refines floors: in a loop a floor also refuses right answers, so it is a brake, not a classifier. | [jevnav](https://github.com/dtduc-git/jevnav), 4 goals × 2 wordings plus 41 real-page cases, 1.13.0, self-graded, opened |
| **E6** | A stated probability is not a hit rate without calibration on your data. Noul stated 79.0% against 72.3% actual, Choice 91.4% against 76.1%, and the 50-95% band was 50-57% correct. Jev returned exactly 1.0 on 56.4% of answers, and 9 of those were wrong. | Challenges any floor that has never been checked against labelled cases. | [Anthus](https://anth.us/blog/can-you-trust-jev-confidence), 8,801 examples, list; [priorbench/jev](https://github.com/priorbench/jev), opened; [jev-certify](https://github.com/nikkoxgonzales/jev-certify), 2,412 answers, 1.13.0, opened |
| **E7** | A Noul and a Choice give different numbers for the same judgment: a Noul and a two-option Choice differed by 0.125 on average over 400 items. On a hidden fair die a Choice put 82.9% on its pick at 19% accuracy while independent Nouls stayed near 1/6, and the bias grew with the number of options. | Refines two-primitive designs: tune each threshold for its primitive and never carry one across a swap. Use a Choice to pick, and a Noul when the probability itself is the output. | [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), 400 items, opened; [jev-does-not-play-dice](https://github.com/KantaHayashiAI/jev-does-not-play-dice), 400 rolls, 1.13.0, list; [jev-noul-vs-choice](https://github.com/TakumiNoguchi2004/jev-noul-vs-choice), 50 trials, 1.13.0, list |
| **E8** | Option order and option set: the sources disagree. Reversing two options flipped 0 of 400 top picks. On six arithmetic problems (432 calls) accuracy was 88.0% with the answer listed first and 57.4% with it fourth. On an ambiguous input, reordering moved the winning probability from 0.62 to 0.48 with the pick unchanged. Adding unrelated options shifted the log-odds between two untouched ones by about 0.28 in one study and 0.31-0.50 in another. | Refines "all candidates in one Choice": clear picks hold, but the number a floor reads on an ambiguous pick depends on order and on what else is listed. | [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), opened; [jev-behavior-study](https://github.com/RINNECODER/jev-behavior-study), 11,621 requests, 1.13.0, opened; [finnhll/jev-eval](https://github.com/finnhll/jev-eval), opened; [jev-wide](https://github.com/123Satyajeet123/jev-wide), 1.13.0, opened; [audio-jevlike](https://github.com/alperiox/audio-jevlike), list |
| **E9** | Wording is the largest lever: criteria rewrites moved paired accuracy from 70% to 96% and from 83% to 100%. A narrow question scored 0.99 where the same check inside a broad question scored 0.30. | Agrees with spending effort on wording first and with asking the narrow question. | [jev-classification-prompting](https://github.com/RastislavDujava/jev-classification-prompting), 41 items, 1.13.0, list; [jev-experimental](https://github.com/SYED-M-HUSSAIN/jev-experimental), 1.13.0, list; [browser-jev](https://github.com/DowLucas/browser-jev), opened |
| **E10** | Splitting helps when the parts are independent signals combined in code: phishing detection scored 62.6% as one question and 95.0% as five signals plus a regression, on a held-out half. It hurts when the parts are one dependent move: action, target and value as three questions split the target 0.55/0.42, while whole moves as options scored 1.00 (one page). | Agrees with `question ▸ option` whole-move labels and with code combining independent answers any-of. | [jev-phishing-bench](https://github.com/anisselbd/jev-phishing-bench), 2,000 emails, 1.13.0, opened; [jevaluate](https://github.com/ElshinQ/jevaluate), 1 page, 1.13.0, opened |
| **E11** | Planted text moves verdicts when it reads as a fact about the judged item, not when it is a command. One added line cut accuracy from 0.965 to 0.265; its false fact alone flipped 24 of 24, its instruction alone 1 of 24. On a command gate, "the owner approved this" let 3 of 30 dangerous commands through, while a blunt "ignore your instructions" let none through but denied about 10% of safe ones. Another study saw 1 flip in 1,056 attacks, so the size of the effect depends on the task. | Agrees with keeping untrusted text out of the source role and making gates demotion-only, and extends that to form agents, where page text in state is untrusted. | [jagged](https://github.com/zkousama/jagged), 486 discussions × 3 repeats, 1.13.0, pre-registered, opened; [jev-engineering](https://github.com/eugeniughelbur/jev-engineering), 300 calls, opened; [decision-injection-bench](https://github.com/cwhy/decision-injection-bench), 1,056 attacks, 1.13.0, list |
| **E12** | Identical requests are near-deterministic but not exact: standard deviation 0.001-0.015, 15 distinct answer sets in 50 identical requests, and one borderline Noul spanning 0.43-0.53 in TypeSafe's own consistency cookbook. A statement and its negation summed to between 0.71 and 1.42. | Agrees that answers near a threshold flip between replays, so replay more than once. | [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), opened; [Noul consistency cookbook](https://docs.typesafe.ai/cookbooks/consistency_noul_cookbook), list; [browser-jev](https://github.com/DowLucas/browser-jev), opened |
| **E13** | The state's language matters more than the instruction's: Russian −11 points on 600 paired items, Korean −6.5, Spanish −3 to −6.4, with calibration usually worse too. Counting and dates: sources disagree, from 99.6% on number and date comparisons to 33.3% on exact counting and 13.2% on sequential state. | Refines voice and chat designs: measure in the caller's language. Neutral on keeping dates and counts in code, which stays a design rule because code is exact either way. | [jev-cyrillic-audit](https://github.com/AHTOOOXA/jev-cyrillic-audit), 600 pairs, 1.13.0, list; [jev-calibration-audit](https://github.com/jujumilk3/jev-calibration-audit), opened; [finnhll/jev-eval](https://github.com/finnhll/jev-eval), opened; [jev-acento](https://github.com/marcosmartinez/jev-acento), 3,200 items, 1.13.0, list; [priorbench/jev](https://github.com/priorbench/jev), opened; [jev-deterministic-benchmark](https://github.com/etsabary/jev-deterministic-benchmark), 1,000 decisions, 1.13.0, list |
| **E14** | Same model, richer state: on 100 generated theory-of-mind scenarios (68 development, 32 held out), stating derived beliefs raised the probability on acceptable answers from 0.51 to 0.87 (development) and 0.53 to 0.88 (held out), while the raw history they were derived from reached only 0.57 and 0.54. A belief that only rules something out stayed low even when stated. Escalating to the richer state when the sparse answer's entropy was higher recovered about 80% of the gain. | Agrees that statements which only rule something out spread probability instead of moving it. On our form pages, per-candidate evidence carried the gain instead of derived statements, and the entropy trigger did not transfer. | [irabkina/tom-jev](https://github.com/irabkina/tom-jev), 100 scenarios, 1.13.0, opened and its tables recomputed from the published data |
| **E15** | Pipecat + Jev, live speech to app actions: on 230 cases (100 clean, 100 with speech-to-text errors, 30 chatter), an LLM and Jev were both perfect on clean input and silent on chatter; on garbled input the LLM returned nothing 39% of the time against Jev's 6%. A "mentioned?" Noul plus a "which value?" Choice per argument, a phonetic matcher for sound-alike names, and a correction accepted only at 60% or more. About 150 ms per request warm and 290 ms after an idle gap. | The design in section 8 of `SKILL.md`. Reading back low-confidence fields is our suggestion; neither source shows one. | Daily's video "Pipecat + Jev: When Speech-to-Text Gets It Wrong", 2026-09-19, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=t74zWhDusns; Pipecat pull requests [#5792](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat/pull/5792) and [#5863](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat/pull/5863) |
| **E16** | Trolley bench: Jev and other open decision models asked the same trolley-problem decisions in several wordings. Rewording the question changed what the models decided. | Agrees that wording is the main lever, and that a decision model is only as good as the evaluations you run on your own wordings. | Runners, raw answers and analysis at [zachlandes/trolley-bench](https://github.com/zachlandes/trolley-bench) |
